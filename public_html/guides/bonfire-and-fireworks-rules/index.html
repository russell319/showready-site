Bonfire and fireworks rules for community events

By Russell Bowers, ShowReady · Last checked 8 November 2026 · England and Wales

Short answer

There is no licence for a bonfire or a fireworks display, but you need a fire risk assessment and should tell the fire and rescue service, the council and nearby residents. Fireworks must stop by 11pm, except midnight on 5 November and 1am on New Year’s Eve, Diwali and Chinese New Year. Professional (category F4) fireworks are for display firms only.

Councils’ own displays for official or national events are exempt from the curfew.

Running a bonfire safely

  • Keep the base to no more than 4 m square and the height to about 3 m;
  • at least 25 m from buildings, trees, fences and overhead cables;
  • a crowd barrier at about 1.5 times the bonfire’s height;
  • build it no more than a week before and check for hedgehogs before lighting;
  • dry untreated wood only: no petrol, paraffin, tyres or plastics.

Running a fireworks display

Using a professional display firm is the safest choice: they bring their own insurance and risk assessment. If you fire them yourself, use only consumer fireworks (categories F2 and F3), follow the safety distances on the box, and don’t let them off within 15 metres (50 feet) of the middle of a road. Storing more than a small quantity needs a licence.

Who to tell

The fire and rescue service, the council (and its events team or Safety Advisory Group if the event is public and large), nearby residents, and your insurer: most policies need bonfires and fireworks declared. On council land, some councils ask for 12 to 16 weeks’ notice and £10 million of insurance.

Common questions

Do I need a licence for a bonfire?

No. There’s no bonfire licence, but you have a legal duty to carry out a fire risk assessment for a public event, and you should tell the fire service, council and neighbours.

What time do fireworks have to stop?

11pm, except midnight on 5 November and 1am on New Year’s Eve, Diwali and Chinese New Year.

Can I run my own fireworks display at a public event?

You can use consumer fireworks (F2 and F3) if you follow the safety instructions, but a professional display firm is strongly recommended for public events, and category F4 fireworks are for professionals only.
