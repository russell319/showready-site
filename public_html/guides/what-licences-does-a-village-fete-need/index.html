What licences does a village fete or summer fair need?

By Russell Bowers, ShowReady · Last checked 8 November 2026 · England and Wales

Short answer

A small fete with no alcohol sales, no amplified music and no road closure often needs no licence at all, just the landowner’s permission, public liability insurance and a risk assessment. Selling alcohol or playing amplified music to the public usually needs a Temporary Event Notice. Food stalls, raffles, road closures, bonfires and fairground rides each add their own permission.

When the council’s events team gets involved

Most councils ask to be told about public events on their land or roads, and many have an event notification form. Larger events (often 500 people or more, or anything with a road closure, fireworks or rides) usually go to the council’s Safety Advisory Group. Notice periods vary from 3 weeks to 16 weeks or more, so check your own council’s page early.

The order to do things in

  1. Get the landowner’s permission in writing.
  2. Check your council’s events page and notice periods.
  3. Arrange public liability insurance.
  4. Give a TEN if you will sell alcohol or have licensable music.
  5. Collect traders’ registration, hygiene rating and insurance.
  6. Write the risk assessment, including fire safety.

Common questions

Does a village fete need a licence?

Not always. A fete with no alcohol sales, no licensable music and no late-night hot food often needs no licence. Selling alcohol or playing amplified music to the public usually needs a Temporary Event Notice.

Do I need a licence to sell cakes at a fete?

Occasional, small-scale home baking for a community event usually doesn’t need registration, but regular or larger food businesses must register with their council 28 days before trading. Ask your council’s food safety team if unsure.

Does a fete need insurance?

There’s no law that says an organiser must have public liability insurance, but landowners and councils almost always ask for it, commonly £5 million or £10 million of cover. Employers’ liability insurance is a legal requirement if you employ anyone.
