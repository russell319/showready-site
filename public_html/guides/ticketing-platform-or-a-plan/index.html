Do you need a ticketing platform or just a plan?

By Russell Bowers, ShowReady · Last checked 19 November 2026 · England and Wales

Short answer

A ticketing site sells your tickets, takes the payments and checks people in. It doesn't tell you which licences your event needs or when to apply. A plan does that part. Many events need both, some need only a plan, and a free event with no capacity limit may not need tickets at all.

What a ticketing platform does

Sites such as Ticket Tailor, TicketSource, Eventbrite and Skiddle sell tickets online, take card payments, send e-tickets, cap numbers and scan people in on the day. Some also bring their own audience, which helps music events and festivals. They charge per ticket or per event, and the fees differ, so compare them for your ticket price.

What a ticketing platform doesn't do

  • Work out whether you need a Temporary Event Notice, a premises licence, a road closure or a Safety Advisory Group.
  • Count your deadlines back from your date for your council.
  • Find your food traders, toilets, first aid or stage, or draw your site plan.

Selling tickets can change what you need

Charging for entry can make an event licensable. Free drinks at a private party that nobody pays to attend aren't usually licensable, but if a drink is included in a ticket price, that counts as selling alcohol. Public entertainment you charge for, such as amplified music, can need a licence or a TEN too. And ticketed public events of 800 or more face new security duties under Martyn's Law, starting no earlier than April 2027. That's why it pays to plan before you put tickets on sale.

Common questions

Does ShowReady sell tickets?

No. ShowReady plans the licences, deadlines and suppliers for your event. If you need to sell tickets, use a ticketing site alongside it.

Do I need tickets for a free event?

Not usually. Tickets help when you need to cap numbers or know who's coming, but a free event can run without them. You may still need licences for a bar, music or food traders.

Does selling tickets mean I need a licence?

It can. A drink included in a ticket price counts as selling alcohol, and charged-for public entertainment can need a licence or a TEN. Check before you put tickets on sale.
