Do I need public liability insurance for an event?

By Russell Bowers, ShowReady · Last checked 8 November 2026 · England and Wales

Short answer

Public liability insurance isn’t a legal requirement for organisers, but councils, landowners and venues almost always ask for it, commonly £5 million of cover and £10 million for higher-risk events such as bonfires, fireworks or fairground rides. Employers’ liability insurance of at least £5 million is a legal requirement if you employ anyone.

What to tell your insurer

Declare anything higher-risk: alcohol, bonfires, fireworks, fairground rides, inflatables, animals, water activities, motorsport, camping, a road closure or drone filming. Ask how volunteers are covered.

Your suppliers’ insurance

Ask every supplier for their own public liability certificate, and check it covers the event date. Ride operators are commonly asked for £10 million per ride; inflatable hire firms, animal providers and fireworks display firms should each have their own cover.

Common questions

Is public liability insurance a legal requirement for an event?

No, but councils, landowners and venues almost always ask for it, so in practice most public events need it.

How much public liability insurance do I need for an event?

Commonly £5 million, and £10 million for higher-risk events such as bonfires, fireworks or fairground rides. Check what your council and landowner ask for.

Do volunteers count for employers’ liability insurance?

It depends on the arrangement. Ask your insurer how volunteers are covered; employers’ liability is required by law if you employ anyone.
