What is a Safety Advisory Group (SAG) and does my event need one?

By Russell Bowers, ShowReady · Last checked 8 November 2026 · England and Wales

Short answer

A Safety Advisory Group is a panel of the council, police, fire, ambulance and other agencies that reviews plans for larger public events. It gives advice; it can’t approve or ban an event, and the organiser keeps the legal duty to run it safely. Events of about 500 people or more, or with road closures, fireworks, rides or camping, usually go to a SAG. Allow 12 to 16 weeks.

Who sits on a SAG

Usually the council’s licensing, environmental health, highways and events teams, with the police, fire and rescue service and ambulance service. Some add the Highways Agency, transport operators or the landowner.

Which events go to a SAG

Each council sets its own threshold. Common triggers are:

  • 500 or more people at once (some councils use 1,000 or 2,000);
  • a road closure or parade;
  • fireworks, bonfires, fairground rides or motorsport;
  • overnight camping;
  • events on council land or with a history of problems.

What to send

An event management plan: the site plan, risk assessment, crowd numbers and how you manage them, stewarding, first aid, traffic and parking, emergency procedures, welfare (toilets, water), and the contacts for the event day. The Purple Guide is the industry reference for what goes in it.

What the SAG can and can’t do

A SAG is non-statutory. It can advise and ask for changes, and its members can use their own powers (for example, the council can object to a licence or refuse land). It can’t approve or prohibit an event on its own, and its advice doesn’t move responsibility away from the organiser.

Common questions

Does my event need to go to a Safety Advisory Group?

If it’s a public event of about 500 people or more, or has a road closure, fireworks, rides or camping, it probably does. Thresholds vary, so check with your council’s events or SAG officer.

How much notice does a SAG need?

Most councils ask for 12 to 16 weeks, and more for large or complex events.

Can a Safety Advisory Group stop my event?

Not by itself. A SAG advises. Its members can use their own powers, such as objecting to a licence or refusing use of council land, and the organiser remains legally responsible for safety.
