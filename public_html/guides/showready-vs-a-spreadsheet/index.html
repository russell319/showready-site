ShowReady or a spreadsheet: which is easier for planning an event?

By Russell Bowers, ShowReady · Last checked 19 November 2026 · England and Wales

Short answer

A spreadsheet is fine for a list of jobs and a budget, and plenty of good events are run from one. What it can't do is tell you which licences your event needs, when each one is due for your council, or update those dates when your numbers or your date change. ShowReady does that part from one description of your event, and you can still download a spreadsheet at the end.

What a spreadsheet does well

  • It's free and you probably already know how to use it.
  • You can lay it out however you like and share it with the committee.
  • It's good for a budget, a stall list and who's doing what on the day.

Where a spreadsheet falls short

  • It doesn't know the rules. You have to find out yourself whether you need a Temporary Event Notice, a premises licence, street trading consent, a road closure or a Safety Advisory Group, usually by ringing round or reading each council's pages.
  • It doesn't know your council's deadlines. A TEN needs 10 clear working days, a road closure can need 4 to 12 weeks, and every council sets its own notice for event applications. You work each date out by hand.
  • Nothing updates on its own. If the crowd grows past 499, or you add a bar or move the date, nothing in the sheet tells you a different licence or an earlier deadline now applies.
  • Everything else lives somewhere else. Risk assessment templates, supplier quotes in emails and texts, the site plan on paper.

When a spreadsheet is enough

For a small private party where nobody pays to get in, nothing is sold, there's no amplified music for the public and nothing happens on a road, you may not need any licences at all, and a list in a spreadsheet will do. As soon as you sell alcohol, charge for entry, put on music for the public, have food traders or use a road or a park, it's worth checking properly.
