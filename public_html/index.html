The part everyone dreads

Licences, councils and deadlines, worked out for your postcode

Small organisers lose events to paperwork they didn't know about. ShowReady checks your event against the rules and counts the deadlines back from your date.

499people

TEN or full licence

Alcohol or music for up to 499 at once can use a Temporary Event Notice. Above that, you need a premises licence.

10working days

TEN deadline

Your notice must reach the council at least 10 clear working days before the event. ShowReady gives you the exact date.

28days

Food trader registration

Every food business must register with its home council at least 28 days before it starts trading. Check it before you take their pitch fee.

800+people

Martyn's Law

Ticketed public events this size face new security duties, starting no earlier than April 2027. ShowReady flags it when it applies.

ShowReady gives guidance to help you plan, not legal advice. Every report links to the authority's own page and names who to confirm with.

Questions

Before you ask

I've never organised an event. Is this for me?

Yes, it's made for you. Pick "This is my first" and you only see the essentials: the licences you need, what to book and a list of jobs in order. Everything else stays out of the way until you want it.

Is this legal advice?

No. ShowReady tells you what usually applies and who decides, with links to each authority's own guidance. Always confirm with the council or authority named in your plan.

Where does it work?

Everywhere in England and Wales: every council area has its own page, forms and deadlines. Scotland and Northern Ireland have different licensing law and will follow later.

What does it cost?

Everything is free during the pilot, which runs until 1 May 2027. After that, planning stays free forever, with optional paid plans from £6 a month for extra tools. See Pricing above for the details.

Does ShowReady take a cut of bookings?

No, never. Suppliers and organisers deal with each other directly and agree their own prices, contracts and payments. ShowReady isn't part of the deal and takes no commission. Paid plans are a flat subscription for extra tools.

How big an event does it handle?

Anything from a garden party to a multi-day festival. The plan changes at the points the law does, such as 200, 499 and 800 people.
